The record in words

A text version of each view below. For every entry as a filterable table with full provenance, see the timeline explorer.

2023 · 43 releases

The first full year after ChatGPT. A handful of labs, often weeks or months between models.

2024 · 94 releases

More than double 2023. New labs enter the record, and the time between releases shrinks.

2025 · 116 releases

The busiest year on record. In some weeks several models arrive at once.

2026 · 60 so far

A partial year, already tracking the 2025 pace.

OpenAI: the acceleration

From about 80 days between releases to about 28. Roughly two months down to three weeks.

Anthropic: the acceleration

A similar compression: about 104 days between releases early on, about 24 lately.

Google DeepMind: the acceleration

From about 58 days to about 22 between releases, the same downward pull.

Alibaba (Qwen): the acceleration

Alibaba's Qwen started fast and stayed fast: about 25 days then, about 11 now. The American labs converged toward a pace it had set.

What the lab said, beside what Epoch measured

Each of 4 releases was graded twice on the same benchmark: once by the releasing lab, once independently by Epoch. Across 5 comparisons the two numbers sit close, 2 of them within a single point, and the gaps run both ways. The record keeps both and averages neither.

Fewer arrive with a paper

In 2023, 51% of releases had a paper on record; by 2026, 12%.

And fewer state their size

In 2023, 70% stated a parameter count; by 2026, 50%. A separate decline, on a different signal.

The record, in two lines

Independent scores rose. Disclosure fell.

What the record shows, in four findings

  • Cadence43 releases in 2023, 116 in 2025. The time between one release and the next keeps shrinking.
  • Independent scoresThe highest independent GPQA Diamond score rose from 36 to 95; SWE-Bench Verified from 31 to 84. Every figure measured by Epoch.
  • Lab beside independent4 releases were graded both ways; 2 of 5 comparisons fall within a single point. Both numbers are kept, neither is averaged.
  • DisclosureReleases arriving with a paper on record fell from 51% to 12%; those stating a parameter count from 70% to 50%.

The record is public, and it maintains itself. It re-checks its sources nightly, drafts candidate entries, and waits for a person to approve them; nothing publishes on its own. Every number carries its provenance, or it does not appear.

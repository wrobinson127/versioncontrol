Version Control

The release record of the AI model era.

Methodology

Version Control is a public release record of AI models from twelve leading AI labs. For each release we track when it shipped, how the public can use it, what it scored on independent evaluations, and whether the lab published a paper, a system card, a model card, and a usage policy.

Four rules apply to every number on this site.

1. Every score names its source

Each benchmark score carries a tag. LAB means the lab reported the score. IND means an independent evaluator measured it. The tag links to the source, and the record stores the source URL, the benchmark name and version, and the date we retrieved it. Every score on the site has one of these tags. The validator refuses any score that arrives without a source, so an untagged number cannot enter the record.

2. We record what a lab did not publish

A release still gets a card when the lab publishes no system card, model card, usage policy, paper, or parameter count. The card says so. Each missing item is marked not disclosed.

That mark means we looked. Someone checked the lab's own sites and found nothing. Where we have not checked yet, the card says not yet researched instead. The two look different on the page and mean different things. "Not disclosed" is a fact about the lab. "Not yet researched" is work we have not done. Only researched releases count toward any percentage on the site.

3. We do not compare scores that are not comparable

A change against the previous model appears only when both scores are on the same benchmark, the same version of it, and from the same kind of source. If the previous model was scored but the pair does not match, the card says no comparable measurement. If the previous model was never scored, the card says nothing, because a missing score and a refused comparison are different things.

All comparisons are computed once, in the data pipeline, and published in a file the site reads. The site does no arithmetic of its own, so what you see cannot drift from what was computed.

4. Benchmark versions are fixed

Every benchmark label carries its version: GPQA Diamond, never "GPQA"; SWE-Bench Verified, never "SWE-Bench". A revised benchmark is a different test. We never draw a line, a delta, or a chart across two versions. Where a lab published its evaluation settings, they appear under the score as written.

Scope

We track twelve labs: OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, Meta, xAI, Mistral, DeepSeek, Alibaba (Qwen), Moonshot AI, Z.ai (Zhipu), Cohere, and Amazon (Nova). The record starts on 2022-11-30, the day ChatGPT launched.

The list can grow. A lab is added when it ships a model that meets Epoch AI's notability bar or holds a top position on major leaderboards over time. Every change to the list is logged with a date and a reason, and the log is never edited. Candidates awaiting review are not visible. An entry is either on the record or it is not.

A release counts if it is a newly named model or version that the public can use: open weights, a public API, or a public service. Language models are in scope, and so are multimodal models that include language, which is why vision-language and audio-language models appear. Not in scope: third-party fine-tunes, quantized copies and reposts, research previews with no public access, and models Epoch AI classifies as neither language nor multimodal. If Epoch AI has left the classification blank, we decide by hand and mark the entry "manual". OpenAI's o3-pro is one such entry.

We record each lab's country as context. It is a filter in the explorer. The story never ranks or groups by it.

How releases are detected

A nightly pipeline watches four independent sources: Epoch AI's models dataset, lab announcement feeds and page changes, model repository activity, and API catalog listings. Candidates that pass correlation and validation are drafted as entries. Nothing is published without a person approving it. Every entry on the record was reviewed by a person before it appeared.

The two benchmarks

We record two benchmarks. Each has a stable, versioned definition maintained by the people who built it, and each is measured independently on an ongoing basis. We do not define the benchmarks. We fix the version and record the measurements.

  • GPQA Diamond: the expert-validated "diamond" subset of GPQA, a graduate-level science benchmark from Rein et al. (2023), built to stay hard even with web access.
  • SWE-Bench Verified: a human-validated subset of SWE-bench (Jimenez et al., 2023), a software-engineering benchmark drawn from real GitHub issues. OpenAI screened the Verified subset in 2024 for tasks that are solvable and well specified.

Scores are percentages from 0 to 100, recorded as the source published them.

Benchmarks decay. As training data grows, a fixed test set can leak into it. We prefer versioned benchmarks run by independent parties, and we watch newer designs built to resist this, such as LiveCodeBench (Jain et al., 2024), which uses problems dated after a model's training cutoff. We watch it; we do not record it. No LiveCodeBench numbers appear on the site or in the dataset.

How each number is calculated

Every headline number is computed from the published files by a stated rule. Nothing is smoothed or modeled.

  • Median gap between releases (the story counter): the median number of days between one release and the next, across all twelve labs, over the 365 days before each release. Because it pools twelve labs, it falls to a few days once the record fills in.
  • Median gap this year (the tile): the same measure, limited to releases in the current calendar year. A different window, stated on each page.
  • Releases per year and per month: raw counts by date. Revisions are counted separately and left out of release counts.
  • Days since the last release: calendar days from the newest release to our last successful update.
  • Releases with a paper link, or a system card link: the number of releases with a link on record for that item.
  • Percentages: every percentage of releases with an item on record divides by the releases we have researched for that item, never by all releases. A release we have not checked is not in the denominator. Each percentage states its denominator.
  • Under review: a column shows "under review" when we have found links but have not yet approved them onto the record. That is our backlog, not the lab's record, and it never shows as a zero or a percentage.

Sources

Release dates, lab names, parameter counts, availability, and the independent benchmark scores come from Epoch AI's datasets, used under CC-BY 4.0:

Epoch AI, "Data on AI Models". Published online at epoch.ai. Retrieved from https://epoch.ai/data/ai-models [online resource].

All independent scores come from one evaluator, Epoch AI's Benchmarking Hub. That is a limit. Every "independent" number on this site is Epoch's measurement, and the lab-versus-independent view compares each lab's own report against Epoch, not against several evaluators. A second evaluator that publishes per-benchmark scores openly would strengthen the record. Few exist. We will not fill the gap with leaderboards that blend several tests into one score, because that is the kind of number this site refuses to show. If a second source appears, its numbers will carry their own tag and will never be averaged with Epoch's. Lab-reported scores cite the lab's own publication.

Where the site says "availability", it is showing Epoch AI's "Model accessibility" field under a shorter name. The values are Epoch's.

Parameter counts are almost all lab-stated. About nine across the whole record are Epoch AI estimates rather than a number the lab published. We keep them as recorded and flag them. The parameter-disclosure figures count whether a release states a parameter count at all.

The decline in parameter disclosure does not depend on those estimates. It holds under every definition we tested, including one that drops every flagged estimate.

Pricing and context length

Pricing and context-length figures carry a "per OpenRouter" label and are display-only. They are not in the downloadable dataset, because OpenRouter publishes no redistribution license for its catalog.

Known limits

  • Lab-reported scores arrive through the nightly pipeline. The backfilled record was built from independent scores, so recent entries may show an independent score before a lab-reported one.
  • Benchmark coverage is thin in the early record, because the two benchmarks did not exist yet or were rarely run. Thin coverage shows as absence, not as zero.
  • Summaries say what the sources support and attribute lab claims to the lab. They do not judge quality.

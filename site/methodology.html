How to read this registry honestly

Version Control is a ledger, not a scoreboard. It records what shipped, what the releasing lab disclosed, and what independent evaluators measured. Four rules govern every number on the site.

1. Every number carries its provenance

Each benchmark value renders with a tag that cannot be separated from it: LAB means the number was reported by the releasing lab, and IND means it was measured by an independent evaluator. The tag links to the source, and the record stores the source URL, the benchmark name and version, and the retrieval date. A number with no source does not appear. There are no exceptions.

2. Non-disclosure is a displayed signal

When a lab publishes no system card, no model card, no usage policy, no paper, or no parameter count, the registry does not leave a blank. It renders an explicit outlined mark: not disclosed. The mark is visually distinct from a zero, from a value, and from data the registry has simply not yet collected. Absence of disclosure is part of the record.

3. Deltas are computed once, and refused when dishonest

A change against the predecessor renders as a delta chip only when the two measurements are comparable: the same benchmark, the same benchmark version, and the same source class. Deltas are computed in the data pipeline and published in a derived file; the site renders that file and never computes a comparison itself. When a predecessor was measured but the pair is not comparable, the registry refuses the comparison and says so in words, in place: no comparable measurement. When the predecessor was never measured at all, nothing renders; silence is not the same signal as refusal.

4. Benchmark versions are pinned, permanently

Benchmark labels always carry their version: GPQA Diamond, never a bare "GPQA"; SWE-Bench Verified, never a bare "SWE-Bench". A revised benchmark is a different measurement axis, and the registry never bridges versions with a delta, a chart, or any other visual. Where a lab's evaluation configuration is known, it renders verbatim beneath the number.

Registry scope

The registry records releases from a closed set of 12 organizations: OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, Meta, xAI, Mistral, DeepSeek, Alibaba (Qwen), Moonshot AI, Z.ai (Zhipu), Cohere, and Amazon (Nova). The record begins on 2022-11-30.

The set can grow. An organization enters the registry when it ships a model meeting Epoch AI's notability bar or sustains top-tier leaderboard presence. Every change to the registry is logged with a date and a reason, and the log is append-only. Pending candidates have no public trace; an entry either exists in the record or it does not.

A tracked release is a new named model or versioned variant that is publicly usable: open weights, public API, or public service. Excluded: third-party fine-tunes, quantizations and reposts, no-access research previews, and models whose domain classification in Epoch AI's dataset includes neither language nor multimodal capability. A multimodal model with a language component is in scope, which is why audio-language and vision-language entries appear in the record.

Detection pipeline

A nightly pipeline watches four independent signals for candidate releases: the Epoch AI models dataset, lab announcement feeds and page changes, model repository activity, and API catalog listings. Candidates that clear correlation and validation are drafted as entries. Nothing publishes without human approval; every entry in the record was reviewed by a person before it appeared.

Benchmark pins

The registry currently records two benchmarks, chosen for stable versioned definitions and sustained independent coverage:

  • GPQA Diamond: graduate-level science questions, diamond subset.
  • SWE-Bench Verified: software engineering tasks, human-verified subset.

Values are percentages on a 0 to 100 scale, recorded as published by their source.

The measures, defined

Every headline number on the site is computed from the published files by a stated rule; nothing is smoothed or modeled.

  • Median gap between releases (the story counter): the median of the gaps, in days, between consecutive releases across all tracked organizations, over a trailing 365 day window evaluated at each release date. It pools twelve organizations, which is why it saturates at a few days once the record densifies.
  • Median gap this year (the tile): the median of the gaps between consecutive releases dated within the current calendar year. A different window from the story counter, stated on each surface.
  • Releases per year and per month: raw counts of release entries by date. Revisions are counted separately and excluded from release cadence.
  • Days since the last release: calendar days from the newest release date to the last successful update.
  • Releases with a paper link, or a system card link: the count of release entries whose disclosure row carries a link on record for that artifact.
  • Under review: a disclosure artifact column renders "under review" when researched links exist in the registrar's queue but none has been approved onto the record. This is the registrar's own state, distinct from a lab's NOT_DISCLOSED, and it is never rendered as a zero or a percentage.

Independent benchmark measurements come from Epoch AI's Benchmarking Hub. Lab-reported figures, where present, cite the lab's own publication.

The OpenRouter posture

Pricing and context-length figures shown on the site, where they appear, carry a "per OpenRouter" attribution and are display-only. They are never included in the downloadable dataset, because OpenRouter publishes no explicit redistribution license for its catalog data.

Known limits

  • Lab-reported benchmark numbers arrive through the nightly pipeline path; the backfilled record is built from independent measurements, so recent entries may show independent numbers before lab-reported ones.
  • Benchmark coverage is sparse in the early record, because the pinned benchmarks did not exist or were not widely run. Sparsity displays as absence, not as zero.
  • Summaries state what the sources support and attribute lab claims to the lab. They carry no evaluation of quality.

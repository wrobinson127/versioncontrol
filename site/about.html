Version Control

The release record of the AI model era.

About

Version Control is a public registry of AI model releases from twelve tracked labs. For each release it records the date, the lab, how the public can use the model, its benchmark scores and where each came from, the change against the previous model where the scores are comparable, and what the lab did or did not disclose. It tracks releases of AI models. (It is not a software version-control tool.)

The registry is built and maintained by Walker Robinson. Each night a script checks four sources for new releases, and a language model drafts an entry for each one. Nothing appears until a person approves it. The record reaches back to 2022-11-30, built from Epoch AI's datasets, and the full dataset is downloadable under CC-BY 4.0. The methodology says exactly where AI tools were used.

The reason it exists: the primary record of a model release is the announcement the releasing lab writes about itself. That post is where most citations lead. The party being measured writes it. It reports whichever benchmarks the lab chose to run. It gets edited after the fact, and sometimes it comes down. What a lab did not say leaves no trace at all. A year on, it is harder than it should be to say what shipped and what was disclosed at the time.

Recording each release when it happens pins it down: the date, the source URL, the retrieval date, the benchmark and its version, and which disclosure documents we could find. For releases before July 2026 we looked in July 2026, so a document published after launch still counts. So: a claim about how fast models are being released can be checked against a count instead of an impression. A claim about disclosure can be checked against the record, including the entries where the answer was nothing, which is the part that usually goes unrecorded. And because the dataset is published in full, a conclusion drawn here can be checked by anyone against the same evidence rather than taken on trust. The methodology page states the rules and the limits. To report an error, open an issue on GitHub.

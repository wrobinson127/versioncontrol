Version Control

The release record of the AI model era.

About

Version Control is a public registry of major AI model releases. For each release it records the date, the organization, the availability class, benchmark measurements with full provenance, the change against the predecessor where the measurements are comparable, and what the lab did or did not disclose. It tracks the versions of frontier AI models, not source code, and it is not an MLOps tool.

The registry is built and maintained by Walker Robinson. A nightly pipeline detects candidate releases from four independent signals and drafts entries; nothing publishes without human review. The record is backfilled to 2022-11-30 from Epoch AI's datasets, and the full dataset is downloadable under CC-BY 4.0.

The reason it exists: the primary record of a model release is the announcement the releasing lab writes about itself. That post is where most citations lead. The party being measured writes it. It reports whichever benchmarks the lab chose to run. It gets edited after the fact, and sometimes it comes down. What a lab did not say leaves no trace at all. A year on, it is harder than it should be to say what shipped and what was disclosed at the time.

Recording each release when it happens pins it down: the date, the source URL, the retrieval date, the benchmark and its version, and which disclosure artifacts existed when the model shipped. Three things follow from that. A claim about how fast models are being released can be checked against a count instead of an impression. A claim about disclosure can be checked against what was on the record at the time, including the entries where the answer was nothing, which is the part that usually goes unrecorded. And because the dataset is published in full, a conclusion drawn here can be checked by anyone against the same evidence rather than taken on trust. The methodology page states the rules and the limits.

About

Every number tells you where it came from, or it does not appear.

Version Control is a public registry of major AI model releases. For each release it records the date, the organization, the availability class, benchmark measurements with full provenance, the change against the predecessor where the measurements are honestly comparable, and what the lab did or did not disclose. It tracks the versions of frontier AI models, not source code, and it is not an MLOps tool.

The registry is built and maintained by Walker Robinson. A nightly pipeline detects candidate releases from four independent signals and drafts entries; nothing publishes without human review. The record is backfilled to 2022-11-30 from Epoch AI's datasets, and the full dataset is downloadable under CC-BY 4.0.

The display rules follow from one commitment: the site shows you the evidence, not a verdict. Provenance tags sit on every number. Non-disclosure renders as an explicit mark rather than a blank. Comparisons that would mislead are refused in words. There are no rankings, no medals, and no scores of the registry's own invention. The methodology page explains each rule.

The name reads three ways, and all three are meant. To a developer, the timeline is a release history: versions committed one after another, each diffed against the last. Literally, the site tracks the versions of AI models and keeps them under control of a public record. And in the older, policy sense of the phrase, a register of what major actors ship, kept neutrally and verifiably, is a monitoring instrument; this one is small, but it is built to those standards.
